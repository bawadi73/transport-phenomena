Three laws.
One equation.

Momentum, heat, and mass transfer are usually taught as separate subjects. They are not. Each says the same thing: a flux is proportional to a gradient, scaled by a diffusivity with units of m²/s. Drag the gradient and watch all three respond identically.

Flat gradient, no flux — regardless of how large the diffusivity is. This is why an insulated wall at steady state carries no heat, and why a fluid in solid-body rotation carries no shear.

Why the minus sign is not a detail

Transport runs down the gradient, never up it. The sign is the second law showing up in a constitutive equation — a reminder that these are not merely curve fits.

Where the analogy earns its keep

Because all three diffusivities share units of m²/s, their ratios are dimensionless and physically meaningful: Pr = ν/α, Sc = ν/DAB, Le = α/DAB. Solve one boundary-layer problem and you have solved all three.
